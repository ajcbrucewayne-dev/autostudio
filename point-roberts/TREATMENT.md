# The Town You Can't Drive To — treatment

Style: **3D Map Explainer** (Lemo-Opuscar). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~48 s, English narration (Kokoro `bm_lewis`, the Off The Map narrator), burned-in subtitles. New this time: Sentinel-2 imagery (10 m), clouds with ground shadows, a living sea (waves, glints, foam).

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Funnel** (space → Pacific Northwest → the peninsula): the style's cliché and the demo's shape.
2. **One drive**: follow a car from Point Roberts through Canada to Blaine; strong, but the "why" (a straight line on a curved coast) gets lost.
3. **The line decides**: open on the peninsula with the 49th parallel already burning across it; the tip below the line glows amber. Then the line's story (1846), then the drive the line forces (two crossings each way), the school run, the 2020 closure, and the question.

**Chosen: 3** with 2's drive as its middle. The signature is the style's *line ignites along its true course*: the 49th parallel lights from the Rockies side to the sea and slices the peninsula.

## 2. Logline and arc
*A straight line drawn in 1846 left 1,200 Americans on a tip of Canada.*
- Setup (0–9 s): a US town you can't reach from the US without leaving it; the line slices the peninsula.
- Turn (9–30 s): 1846, the 49th parallel; the drive through Canada (two border crossings each way); kids' four crossings a day.
- Ending (30–48 s): 2020, the border closes and the town is cut off by land; the question.

## 3. Benchmark
- News 3D terrain explainers: one claim per screen, labels that hand over with height. Don't take colours, icons, typefaces or camera paths.
- Road-trip map animations (a glowing route that drives itself): learn the satisfying read of a route; don't take their designs.

## 5. Sound
