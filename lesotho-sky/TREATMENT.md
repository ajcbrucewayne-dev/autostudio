# Kingdom in the Sky — treatment

Style: **3D Map Explainer** (Lemo-Opuscar). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~46 s, English narration (Kokoro `bm_lewis`, the Off The Map narrator since "One Clock"), burned-in subtitles.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Funnel** (space → southern Africa → Lesotho → out): the style's cliché, and the shape of the demo.
2. **Lowest to highest**: a journey up the Senqu (Orange) river from the lowest point (1,400 m) to the summit (3,482 m). Good motion, but the "surrounded" fact gets lost.
3. **The rising sea**: open on Lesotho already an island in a flooded world (sea +1,345 m, Britain's highest peak); drain it to show South Africa around it; then let the sea rise from 0 to 1,400 m over the whole region while a counter measures what drowns. Lesotho never gets wet.

**Chosen: 3**, closing with 2's climb to the summit and the history beat. The sea level is a measuring instrument the viewer reads without numbers: the water line itself is the data layer.

## 2. Logline and arc
*Raise the sea 1,400 metres and four-fifths of South Africa disappears; Lesotho doesn't even get wet.*
- Setup (0–9 s): the island in a flooded world → drained → an enclave inside South Africa.
- Turn (9–26 s): the sea rises; 43 % of South Africa under at 1,000 m, ~79 % at 1,400 m; Lesotho dry. Highest low point on Earth; peak 3,482 m.
- Ending (26–46 s): why it's a separate country (1868 protection, 1966 independence); the sea rises again; "Kingdom in the Sky"; the question.

## 3. Benchmark
- News 3D terrain explainers: learn the single-quantity-per-screen discipline and labels that hand over with height. Don't take colours, icons, typefaces, camera paths.
- Sea-level-rise visualisations (a water plane climbing over relief): learn the satisfying, physical read of a rising waterline. Don't take their imagery, music or shots.

## 5. Cue map / sound
D dorian at 92 bpm (bar ≈ 2.61 s). Hook: bowed vibraphone + low pedal; a hit when the water drains and the border ignites. Rising sea: a slow string-and-harp ostinato that climbs a step every 200 m; a soft water swell; tick per 100 m on the counter; a chime when Lesotho is reached by the counter (1,400 m) — silence before it. Summit: horn swell. History: frame drum, low cellos. Ending: harp arpeggio, resolve.
Ambience: high wind over mountains, water lapping (filtered noise) during the floods.
