# One Clock — treatment

Style: **3D Map Explainer** (Lemo-Opuscar). Vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~44 s, English narration, burned-in subtitles.
For: Off The Map (YouTube Shorts). Narration: Kokoro `bm_lewis` (a deep, calm British documentary voice, new to this channel).

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Funnel** — orbit → China → Kashgar → back to orbit. Clear, but it is the style's cliché (and its demo's shape).
2. **Before / after on one terrain** — five clocks lit over China, then one. Strong for the hook, but it ends where it starts: no journey, no place.
3. **Chase the sunrise** — one winter morning. The real dawn line crosses China from the Pacific to the Pamirs while a single Beijing-time clock rolls from 06:48 to 10:12. The viewer *feels* four hours of sunrise happening under one unchanged clock.

**Chosen: 3, opened with 2's hook.** The film's argument is a physical one (the sun moves, the clock doesn't), and a 3D terrain lit by a real, moving low sun is the one thing this medium does that our flat map engine can't. Opening image: China already lit with five zones (no orbit dive). Ending: held over Kashgar on the last quantity, two clocks, then a crane up that shows the east in full day while the west is still at dawn.

## 2. Logline and arc

*On a winter morning the sun takes four hours to cross China, and every clock in the country says the same thing the whole time.*

- **Setup** (0–10 s): China "should" have five zones; it has one; it's over 5,000 km wide.
- **Turn** (10–29 s): it really did have five until 1949; the one clock goes back to Beijing. Then we ride the dawn west: cities light up one by one while the Beijing clock keeps running.
- **Ending** (29–44 s): Kashgar's sun rises after 10 a.m.; locals keep their own unofficial clock, two hours behind. One country, one clock. The question to the viewer.

## 3. Benchmark

- **News 3D terrain explainers** (a regional dive to the point that matters): learn the funnel discipline, labels that hand over with height, one quantity per screen. Don't take: their colours, icons, typefaces, camera paths.
- **Time-lapse "day/night" visualisations of the Earth** (the terminator crossing continents): learn the drama of a real, curved dawn line and long raking shadows. Don't take: their imagery, music, any specific shot.

## 6. Cue map

- Tempo 96 bpm (0.625 s/beat), 4/4. Hook on its own (free time) until the "one" hit; grid starts at 3.9 s.
- Bars: shot 2 = 4 bars (pulse: low pizzicato + sine pedal); shot 3 = 4 bars (pad: bowed vibraphone/strings), a half-bar silence before the horn swell; shot 4 = 8 bars, ostinato in 8ths then 16ths (accelerando by subdivision, not tempo), city chimes on the beats nearest each dawn crossing; shot 5 = 5 bars, pedal + glock; shot 6 = 4 bars, resolve on the tonic with a frame-drum downbeat on the last label.
- Key sync points: "one" hit 2.6 s ± 0; Kashgar landing on a downbeat (~26.4 s); end card on a downbeat.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |

| Ending | air thinning | — | resolve |

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles: map-legend shelf bottom-left, amber vertical rule, one English line (no Chinese line: the film is single-language), never wrapped, Barlow Condensed 600. Map labels keep out of the bottom 22 %.
- Labels: country/sea names Barlow Condensed 600, wide tracking; cities 500. Numbers 600 at 80–110 px with a small unit. Sources line in a corner: "Terrain: AWS Terrain Tiles (SRTM/GMTED) · Imagery: NASA Blue Marble · Borders: Natural Earth 1:10m, China view · Relief ×4 · Sunrise times computed for 21 Dec".
- Every map shot includes all of China (Taiwan, Hainan, South China Sea islands with the dashed line where in frame), per DIRECTOR §13.
